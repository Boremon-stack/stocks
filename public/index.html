Volatility-managed investing · NSE

Hold less when the market is shaking. Measure whether it helps.

Every month, ₹10,000 goes into two plans on the first trading day of the month. One plan invests it at once. The other sets how much of its money sits in the index from last month's volatility, and keeps the rest in cash. We run both on NSE's official Nifty 50 closes, 2020 to 2025. It is a practical adaptation of Moreira and Muir, Volatility-Managed Portfolios (SSRN 2659431), not an exact replication.

Method

How the volatility plan works

Both plans get the same money on the same days. The differences from the paper are listed on the results page.

Add the money

On the first trading day of each month, ₹10,000 is added to both plans at that day's close.

contribution = ₹10,000 each month

Measure last month

Add up the squared daily index moves of the previous calendar month. Only that month is used.

RV = sum of r squared, previous month

Set the share in the index

Compare last month's inverse variance with the average of earlier months. The share is capped at 100% of the money, and the rest stays in cash.

target = min( (1/RV) / average earlier (1/RV), 100% )

The first two months have no earlier estimate, so their target is 0. Cash earns nothing, and prices exclude dividends.

Monthly DCA

The ₹10,000 is invested in the index at once on the first trading day of each month. Nothing is held in cash.

Volatility-managed

On the same days, the plan rebalances to the target share of its total money, shares plus cash. The rest stays in cash.
